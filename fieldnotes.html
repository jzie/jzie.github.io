Field Notes: Android Automotive on a motorcycle

What changes when you take a platform built for cars and put it on a vehicle with one screen, no way to interact while moving, and warning lights the law requires to work instantly. Written for anyone at a manufacturer weighing the same question, and updated every session of the build.

How to read this

Every entry carries one of three labels, so nothing here reads as more certain than it is.

  • Confirmed  I saw it myself on this setup, and the linked session note shows how.
  • Documented  Stated in Google's own documentation or another public source.
  • Assumed  My reasoning, not yet tested. Treat with suspicion.

Everything here comes from public sources and my own motorcycle. Nothing internal to any manufacturer.

1. The differences that matter

One display, not twoConfirmed, Day 2

The platform assumes a central touchscreen plus a cluster behind the wheel. A motorcycle has a cluster and nothing else, so everything the platform does with a main screen has nowhere to go. Day 2

Nothing to touch while movingAssumed

A car driver can safely touch a screen at a red light. A rider wears gloves, has both hands loaded and has no passenger to hand things to. Any interaction that is not available while parked is effectively unavailable.

Warning lights are legally required, and instantDocumented

Turn signals, high beam, neutral, oil pressure, ABS and the engine light must appear the moment the key turns. Android takes tens of seconds to start, so it cannot be the only thing rendering that screen.

Android is not a safety-certified systemDocumented

Android Automotive holds no automotive safety certification such as ISO 26262, and manufacturers isolate it from safety-critical functions, often running it alongside a real-time operating system. Google's own architecture splits the cluster: a high availability renderer draws the safety and regulatory elements, and the Android layer draws everything else.

The data model is car-shapedConfirmed, Day 2

Gear arrives as park, reverse, neutral and drive. A motorcycle has neutral and six gears. Lean angle, chain condition and similar do not exist in the platform at all. Day 2

The environment is harsherAssumed

Direct sunlight, rain, engine vibration and temperatures from freezing to baking, with no cabin around any of it. All of it affects readability, mounting and component life.

Every euro is fought overAssumed

A cluster is a far larger share of the bill of materials on a €6,000 motorcycle than on a €40,000 car, and there is less electrical power to spare.

2. Changes we made

Treated the single display as the whole clusterConfirmed, Day 2

Rather than the platform cluster APIs, which assume a second screen, we run one full-screen app. Cheaper and simpler, and it costs us the platform's own cluster integration. Decision

Built a custom layout instead of using Google's templatesConfirmed, Day 2

The Car App Library gives driver-distraction checks for free but only allows lists, grids and navigation screens. A cluster is a few very large numbers, so we took the custom route and the responsibility that comes with it. Decision

Converted units in the display layerConfirmed, Day 1

The platform stores speed in metres per second and the app decides what the rider sees. Keeping that split means a change of units never touches the data path. Day 1

Subscribed to values rather than pollingConfirmed, Day 2

Lower power, faster updates, and the current value arrives on subscribing, so the cluster is populated at power-on rather than blank. Decision

3. Limitations found

Gear cannot express a motorcycle gearboxConfirmed, Day 2

The standard gear value has no way to say third of six.

No motorcycle-specific values existDocumented

Nothing for lean angle, or anything else particular to two wheels. Custom vendor values are the only route.

A frozen value looks exactly like a steady oneConfirmed, Day 2

The platform delivers changes, so silence and stillness are indistinguishable. On a cluster that is dangerous.

Cluster APIs assume a second displayDocumented

The platform's cluster path expects the manufacturer to provide a service driving a secondary display, behind a dedicated permission. On a one-screen vehicle that design does not fit.

Start-up time cannot meet the key-turn expectationAssumed

Not yet measured on real hardware. Currently the single biggest untested risk in the project.

4. Workarounds

Split the clusterPlanned

A small, instant safety layer owns the regulated warning lights and Android owns everything else. This mirrors how the industry already handles it, and it is the heart of the feasibility question.

Custom vendor values for motorcycle dataPlanned, Phase 3

Define our own properties in the vendor range for gear, RPM and the rest, which requires a custom Android build rather than a stock image.

Staleness timeout in the appPlanned

Blank a value when it is older than a set age, rather than showing a number that stopped being true.

Start by itself at power-onPlanned

Nobody taps an icon on a motorcycle.

5. Open questions

  • How long from power-on to a usable Android cluster on real hardware?
  • Does the split cluster hold up, or does the safety layer end up drawing so much that Android adds little?
  • Is the R3's diagnostic port usable in listen-only mode, or does it only answer when asked?
  • What does this add to the bill of materials, and what does it draw?
  • Is an affordable display readable in direct sunlight?

6. Verdict

Empty until the criteria are written and the prototype runs. I would rather publish a no, with the reasons, than quietly stop.
